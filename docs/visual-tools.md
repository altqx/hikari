# Visual tools: the rail, the shared transform and the tool interface

T1 ([#176](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/issues/176)) lays the ground the visual families T2-T6 build on. It follows [media.md](media.md) (tool state and handles kept apart from the decoded textures, one shared transform with its inverse), the accepted layout A of [reviewed-surface-layouts.md](ux/reviewed-surface-layouts.md) (a named rail beside the Video canvas, values below it) and the transaction rule of [edit-transactions.md](proposals/edit-transactions.md) and [surface-decision-routing.md](proposals/surface-decision-routing.md) (accepted on [#55](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/issues/55)).

## The transform

`VideoView` works in the video window's device pixels, as legacy did, and keeps legacy's float steps and integer truncations: the fixtures replay the legacy probe's capture (`tests/fixtures/legacy-observations/local-t1-visual-20261005`) float for float, but for the approved departures T1-copy-coords-view and T1-wheel-zoom-stale ([compatibility-decisions.md](compatibility-decisions.md)). The host feeds it the panel's logical size and the window's device pixel ratio (`toDevice` / `toLogical`), the video's encoded size and SAR (the media helper's Open answer, so a decoder failure keeps the geometry), the script's PlayRes and the aspect override.

- `videoRect()` is where the frame is drawn (m_BackBufferRect) and `sourceRect()` the part of the frame drawn there (m_MainStreamRect, the zoom). The presenter takes both, so the frame, the subtitles and the tools agree.
- `scriptToView` and `viewToScript` are legacy's GetCalculatedInPos / GetCalculatedOutPos, the tools' transform and its inverse for hit testing. They use the zoom as of the last `refreshToolTransform()` (legacy SetVisualZoom), which every zoom change, resize, tool change and edit runs. Legacy's wheel zoom and zoom toggle skipped it, leaving the tools unzoomed until the next of the others; the approved departure T1-wheel-zoom-stale refreshes them.
- **Zoom and pan** (FA12 [#569](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/issues/569)) follow `video.zoomModel`. Under `ZoomModel::Aegisub35`, the default, the whole picture is scaled (0.125× to 10×) and panned as in Aegisub 3.5's VideoDisplay: `sourceRect()` stays the whole frame and `frameRect()` is where the presenter draws it, which may lie partly outside the panel (the stage clips it). The tools follow through `zoomScale` and `zoomMove`, so `scriptToView` / `viewToScript` stay exact while zoomed and panned. Ctrl+wheel zooms around the pointer, Shift+wheel pans 5 px a notch, the middle button drags the picture and a pinch zooms; `video.scrollAction`, `video.ctrlScrollAction` and `video.shiftScrollAction` choose each wheel's action. A tool keeps the wheel combinations that `takesWheel(shift, control)` claims (all-tags, drawing and vector clip) and the middle button when `takesMiddleButton()` (rotation XY, scale). For the other tools a middle press waits for 3 px of movement before it pans, and a click that does not move reaches the tool, so the crosshair's middle click still positions. `ZoomModel::Legacy` keeps HikariSub legacy's crop zoom described above. GLOBAL_RESET_VIDEO_ZOOM, "Reset zoom and pan", resets both in either model.
- The crosshair keeps its own coefficients (VisualCross.cpp:80-92), legacy's, which differ from `viewToScript` by design (`crossCoefficients`, `crossScriptPoint`). VIDEO_COPY_COORDS copies the crosshair's position under the pointer as "x,y" (approved departure T1-copy-coords-view; legacy scaled by the whole window less one pixel, VideoBox.cpp:1395-1412).

## Adding a tool (T2-T6)

A family's card adds one class and one line; the rail, the routing and the drawing need no change.

1. Implement `visual::VisualTool`: `family()`, `pointer()`, `overlay()`, and as needed `reset()` (legacy SetCurVisual: the view, script or active Line changed), `key()` (true when used), `takesKey()` (VT2: the keys it takes ahead of the bindings, its nudge's arrows), `values()` / `setValue()` (the numeric alternative in the inspector bar: a typed value commits, a scrub's `ValueEdit::Sample` stages), `options()` / `setOption()` (the family's own row, below), `previewLines()` (Lines the video renders after the Document's) and `warnsOutsideLine()` (true but for the crosshair).
2. Return it from `makeVisualTool()` in `src/application/visual_tools.cpp`.

The host gives a tool only what legacy's Visuals got: pointer events in device pixels over the video while a video is open, the Video panel's keys that no Video binding takes (and, first, the keys `takesKey()` claims: [the arrow nudge](#tool-hotkeys-and-the-arrow-nudge-vt2)), and resets. A tool other than the crosshair gets nothing outside its Line's time or on a comment (Visuals::Draw's blockevents); the host draws the warning centred on the video unless VIDEO_VISUAL_WARNINGS_OFF (approved departure T1-warning-centre).

Every edit goes through a gesture:

- `host.beginGesture(host.batchTargets(), history)` on press (or a nudge's key press). The targets are fixed then: the batch picker's Lines when some are picked, else the active Line. Changing the picker, selection or active Line later never retargets it. It is refused for a protected session, a Document a macro holds, or targets that are gone.
- `gesture->before(line)` is the target as the gesture began (the pending draft applied); `stage(line, text, translation)` replaces the staged text on every sample. Nothing reaches the Document, the draft or the history meanwhile.
- `host.commitGesture()` on release: one history step named `history` (`familyInfo(family).history`, legacy SubsFile.cpp:228-238), after the pending draft's own step. Without staged changes nothing is recorded; a gesture whose Document changed since it began is refused.
- Esc during the gesture (the host handles it) drops it and leaves the pre-gesture draft.
- A change of the active Line or of the editing target drops an open gesture the same way, and the tool is reset to the new Line (legacy's SetVisual dropped the tool's unsent preview). The gesture is never moved to the new Line.

Draw with `Overlay` (lines, circles, text, and filled polygons below or above the lines) in device pixels of the video window; the host converts to logical coordinates and paints it apart from the frame. Tests can swap in a tool with `VisualToolsController::setTool`.

## Tool hotkeys and the arrow nudge (VT2)

VT2 ([#270](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/issues/270), owner-approved audit [issuecomment-6307366142](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/issues/270#issuecomment-6307366142)) gives each tool a Video command and moves the nudge to the arrows. Legacy had no key to choose a tool (VideoToolbar::OnMouseEvent only).

**The tool commands.** `FamilyInfo::hotkey` names each family's command (`familyOfHotkey` reads it back); `VisualToolsController::runHotkey` chooses the family as its rail button does, so the on tool's key goes back to the Position pointer. The commands are Video bindings (`hotkeys.cpp`, ids 2022-2032 and VT3's 2033, named as the rail names the tools), listed, rebound and imported as the others; they act while the Video panel is focused or hovered (KB1's router) and in fullscreen, never while a text field has the focus. The rail's tooltip shows the key ("Text scaling (S)").

**The audit.** Each key is checked against every default binding of the five contexts and the keys the Video panel takes in code (F full screen, B and Esc leaving it, Menu, Return in the zoom mode, Ctrl+Shift+Z). `shell_tests` everyDefaultBindingReachesItsCommand (KB3's audit) lists the ten that are legacy defaults elsewhere (and VT3's Q) as the Video window's departures.

**The vector clip modes** (FA9, [#566](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/issues/566)). Each button on the vector clip's row has a Video command with no default key: VIDEO_VECTOR_CLIP_DRAG, _LINE, _BEZIER, _BSPLINE, _POINT and _DELETE (2034-2039) choose a mode, and VIDEO_VECTOR_CLIP_INVERT (2040) toggles Invert clip. `vectorClipOptionOfHotkey` maps them to the row's options. ## What T2 added for the families

T2 ([#177](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/issues/177), Position and Move: `visual_position.h`) needed more of legacy's `Visuals` than the crosshair did. These are shared by the later families:

- `visual_script.h`: what `Visuals` read from the script. `ScriptState` holds the Document, its Styles, SubsSize, the video's time and Timebase, the active Line as the Line editor holds it (the pending draft applied) and the text measure. Beside it are `linePosition` (GetPosition: the first `\pos` or `\move`, the tag's place, the default position with the stacking of unpositioned Lines), `posnScale` (GetPosnScale on the editor text, with its 7-value table kept by the tool between calls), `moveTimes` (GetMoveTimes from the video's frames), `calcMovePosition`, `textSize` (GetTextSize and GetDrawingSize through `TextMeasurePort`, legacy's swapped descent and leading kept), `changeText` / `replaceTag` (TagFindReplace's ChangeText and Replace) and the handles (`drawRect`, `drawCircle`, `drawCross`, `drawArrow`, `drawDashedLine`, `drawHelperLine`) in legacy's colours.
- `VisualHost` gives the video's time (`videoTimeMs`, VideoBox::Tell), its `timebase`, the `textMeasure` (the Qt port of GetLineTextExtents), the Grid's `ignoreFiltered` and `log` (HikariLog).
- `VisualTool` can override `warning()` (Position draws its warning only while none of its Lines is visible), `selected()` (legacy made a new tool on every family change, so its own state starts over; the toolbar's options stay), `blocked()` (the host calls it when it renders, or takes a pointer event, while the tool is blocked: legacy's Draw ran there, and Position's ends a helper-cross drag) and `options()` / `setOption()`: the rail's second row (legacy VideoToolbar's VisualItem), shown by `VisualToolOption.qml` in the inspector bar as toggles with their K1 icon and choices.
- `Pointer` has `rightDown` and the `DoubleClick` kind (legacy's LeftDClick; Qt sends it after the second press).
- The host shows the open gesture's staged texts on the video (`setPreview`, legacy's dummy rendering) and the committed Document after it; Esc resets the tool from its Lines.
- Keys reach the tool whether or not its pointer events are blocked (VideoBox::OnKeyPress, VideoBox.cpp:666-668).
- The pointer's shape (the user's 2026-10-13 decision to port legacy's): a tool calls `host.setPointerShape()` where legacy called `tab->video->SetCursor()`, and `host.hasArrow()` where it tested `HasArrow()`; `edgePointerShape()` is the edge-and-corner expression of the rectangle tools. The shape is the host's, as legacy kept it on the video window: the host puts the arrow back when the family changes (VideoBox::OnChangeVisual), when the tool is blocked (Visuals::Draw) and on Esc, and `VisualToolsController::pointerShape` gives the overlay the Qt cursor (SIZEWE, SIZENS, SIZENWSE, SIZENESW and SIZING as SizeHor, SizeVer, SizeFDiag, SizeBDiag and SizeAll). The zoom mode's and fullscreen's cursors and the crosshair's hidden pointer take precedence. One departure: Scale's Shift changes the shape only while a button is down (T3-scale-shift-hover, User, 2026-10-13); legacy also put a drag's shape up when Shift was pressed or released while hovering.
- A tool decides when it reads its Lines again: legacy set Position again after its commits (SetModified, ShowEditOnVideo) but not Move (EditBox::Send and the batch's SetModified are dummies), so Move ignores the host's refresh after its own commit.

A family's options use the K1 roles the manifest names (frame-to-scale, scale-x, scale-y, two-points for T2); `VisualToolOption.qml` lists the roles the families' options show literally, so the icon test sees the roles placed; a family adding a toggle adds its role there, and `shell_tests`' `visualToolOptionIconsFollowTheModel` fails for any toggle whose shown role is not the one its `ToolOption::iconRole` gives.

## The inspector bar (VT1)

VT1 ([#269](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/issues/269)) merges T1-T6's options row, values row and the readout beside the video times into one bar below the canvas, `VisualToolBar.qml`; fullscreen's bar shows the same bar as its top row (FS1's `inspectorSlot`, while a tool other than the crosshair is on), after FS1 (#275) moved the families into its Visual tools menu and the rail. The tool's options and values carry a `group` (`ToolOption::group`, `ToolValue::group`), and the bar draws consecutive items of a group after its label, translated in the bar's table (context HikariSub.VisualTools; the tools' own texts stay legacy's). A value has a `unit` shown inside its field and a scrub `step` per pixel.

- **Typed and scrubbed values.** `VisualToolsController::setValue` is Enter's and a scrub's release: `VisualTool::setValue(..., ValueEdit::Commit)`, one history step. `scrubValue` is a scrub's sample: `ValueEdit::Sample` opens the tool's gesture on the first sample and stages each later one on it (the video shows them, the Document waits); Esc runs the controller's `escape()`, which cancels it and resets the tool. A tool refuses a value while a gesture it did not open for a value is running, and a value that is not a plain number within ±1e8 (`parseValue`, `kValueLimit`). A refused sample (Scale below zero) is not taken: the field keeps the last accepted one and the release commits that; a refused release drops the scrub's gesture as Esc does. A step the session refuses (a blocked draft) resets the tool, so its handles and fields go back to the text's values. With "Change all scale tags" on, a Scale value whose ratio to the press's scale is not a finite number is refused: on an axis at zero the ratio is undefined (legacy's drags write nan there), and from a tiny scale in the text it overflows (it would write inf). A typed angle is held in 0-360 for any number of turns. After an edit over several Lines, the rotations read the active Line again, so the fields and the next edit start from its angles. The crosshair's readout and Copy keep the last position when the pointer leaves the video, and a reset of the tool drops them with the cross. Move's typed times go into every picked Line, each from its own start, and count from the Line editor's start (its pending draft's). They are whole milliseconds, written and shown exactly (not through a float, which rounds past 2^24 ms). Over several Lines a typed time leaves each target's other time as its tag wrote it (a drag writes wxAtoi's whole part of both, as legacy). The Video panel's bar and fullscreen's share one controller, so only the bar in the window showing the tools has the dialogs (the notice, the shape and tag editors): an open one moves to fullscreen as it opens and back as it closes. Typed text not yet applied is dropped when the Document, the active Line or the picked Lines change (the controller's `editTarget`), so Enter never writes one Line's text into another; the tool's own refreshes (a frame, the pointer) leave it.
- **Which values.** Position's X and Y; Move's Start and End points and their times (from the Line's start in ms, step 10); Scale's X and Y (%); RotationZ's Z angle and RotationXY's X and Y angles (°), each with Origin X and Y. The crosshair's X and Y are read-only, and its Copy video position action copies them in VIDEO_COPY_COORDS' "x,y" form. Scale's and the rotations' typed values go through the same state their drags set (the arrows, the angles, `m_org`), so "change all" and "preserve proportions" act on a typed value as on a drag. One difference: over several Lines with "change all" on, legacy's drags scale both axes by the larger of the two ratios, but a typed or scrubbed Scale value scales each axis by its own ratio, so a value below the press's applies to that axis alone (`m_valueRatios`).
- **One line for every tool (VB1).** The bar is one line, so a tool switch does not resize the video; the crosshair has none (its X, Y and Copy video position are on the times line, `VisualPointerReadout.qml`), and the bar hides for it. The bar lays the tool's groups out unseen, each whole, and shows as many as fit, less the picker's slot for a batch tool; the rest fold, last first, under More options, a popover holding them live. The unseen copies are made again only when the groups change (their structural string), have no object names and take no focus.
- **Not rebuilt during a scrub.** The bar's value groups derive from a structural string of the values' names, groups, labels, units and editability, so a sample changes the fields' texts but not the fields; each field looks its value up by name. The option groups likewise derive from the options' names, kinds, icons, tooltips, groups and list ends; each option looks its checked, enabled, entries and chosen entry up by name. So a toggle, or Copy video position enabled with the crosshair's first position, makes no item again, and a value field keeps its place. Windows CI on 3912e5aca once read a newly made, not yet placed readout.

## Scale and the rotations (T3)

T3 ([#178](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/issues/178)) adds `ScaleTool` (`visual_scale.h`), `RotationZTool` and `RotationXYTool` (`visual_rotation.h`), ports of legacy Scale, RotationZ and RotationXY (VisualScale.cpp, VisualRotationZ.cpp, VisualRotationXY.cpp) with their toolbar items (ScaleItem, RotationZItem, RotationXYItem). What they share with their legacy bases, Visuals and TagFindReplace, is in `visual_transform.h` (namespace `visual::transform`): GetPosnScale, GetMoveTimes, CalcMovePos, GetPosition with GetDialogueAdditionalPosition, GetTextSize, ChangeOrg, FindTag, Replace, ReplaceAll, ChangeText, getfloat and the clip rewrites. T2's helpers port some of the same legacy functions; the two stay apart until the families share one module.

- **Two paths, as legacy's Visuals::SetVisual.** A gesture whose targets are the active Line alone edits the editor's text: the tag goes into the block at the editor's caret (FindTag's mode 0; the host's `editorSelection`, the Line editor's caret in the raw text), each sample from the previous one, and the caret is put at the tag afterwards (`setEditorSelection`, only when the commit went through; a refused commit leaves the caret and the tool reads the unchanged text again). Any other target set (the batch picker's Lines) takes legacy's several-Line path: each target's text through ChangeVisual(txt, dial, n) from position 0, recomputed for the commit.
- **Options.** `options()` and `setOption()` are the rail's second row (`VisualToolOption.qml` in the inspector bar below the canvas, grouped under Rectangle and Tags for Scale, Tags for the rotations): legacy's icons (K1's frame-to-scale, scale-x, link, scale-y, original-frame, tool-scale-rotation, resample, two-points), help texts as tooltips, the items' greying as disabled buttons and their links (the rectangle or "preserve proportions" switch "change all" on; the aspect ratio switches width on; RotationZ's "preserve proportions" switches "change all" on). A family's toggles stay when the family changes; its other state starts over (`selected`, legacy Visuals::Get).
- **No reset after their own step.** The host resets a tool on every new revision; these three return true from `keepsStateAfterCommit()`, so the controller counts their own step's revision as seen. Legacy sent these edits with the visual dummy flag and ran no SetVisual after them (Visuals.cpp:791-829, SubsGridBase.cpp:1125-1157), so the tool kept the caret at the tag, its angles, its press point and its rectangle.
- **A release that changes nothing.** A release that leaves every target's text and extradata as they were (the two-point angle's first click, a click off both points, a click that writes the values the Lines already hold) cancels the gesture: no step, and the Document stays unmodified, on both paths (T3-release-no-change, the rule T6's tools follow; legacy's SetVisual(false) committed on every release).
- **Esc.** With a gesture open Esc drops it and the tool reads the unchanged text again (its handles go back). With none, Esc drops a tool's pending step (`cancelPending`): the first point of RotationZ's two-point angle, the card's evidence; legacy had no key for it.
- **Drawing.** Legacy's Direct3D shapes in device pixels: Scale's three arrows (DrawArrow) or its rectangles, RotationZ's ring, angle handle and \org cross or its two points, RotationXY's grid, axes and arrow cones projected as its matrices set up (`projectXY`, `drawXYGrid`: D3DXMatrixRotationYawPitchRoll, LookAtLH from z = -17.2, PerspectiveFovLH at 120 degrees, translated to \org in clip space, clipped at the near plane). Their colours are legacy's fixed overlay colours, not settings.
- **Evidence.** The `legacy_visual_t3_capture` probe ([local-t3-visual-20261005](../tests/fixtures/legacy-observations/local-t3-visual-20261005/README.md)) runs the legacy tools unchanged; `VisualCapture.ReplaysTheLegacyT3Probe` replays every case and compares every Line, the editor's text and caret, the commits (SetModified and Send against the history steps), the log and the handles exactly; `VisualOverlay` checks the ring against RotationZ::DrawVisual's formulas and the X/Y grid against the D3DX pipeline worked by hand. `hikari_backends_visual_geometry_tests` renders the tools' edits with libass and checks the ink turns about the \org the tool draws and scales away from the position its arrows start from.

## The tool's own row, the preview and the notices

A family's own buttons (legacy VideoToolbar's items for the toggled family, VisualItem) are `VisualTool::options()` and `setOption()`: a toggle (a mode), a choice, or an action (a button that acts at once). `VisualToolOption.qml` shows them in the inspector bar before the values, with their K1 icon and legacy's help text (a choice in a group as a text dropdown); a role a family uses must be named on its `iconRole` line so `icon_tests` sees it placed.

While a gesture is open the video shows its staged texts, and a tool's `previewLines()` (the vector clip's mask) after the Document's Lines: legacy's "dummy" rendering (Visuals::RenderSubs, AppendClipMask). The host builds that Document (`VisualToolsController::subtitles`, and `setPreview` when it changes); nothing reaches the Document or its history.

A tool commits through the host; the clips, as T3's three, keep their state afterwards (`keepsStateAfterCommit()`): legacy never reset them after their own edit (EditBox::Send with visualdummy and SetModified with dummy skip ShowEditOnVideo's SetVisual, SubsGridBase.cpp:1147-1149), so the controller treats the revision their own commit made as seen. Another edit, an undo or another active Line resets the tool (legacy SetCurVisual); a cancelled gesture (Esc) resets it too, so its points and corners go back to the Line.

A notice legacy showed in a message box (the vector clip's "Double m" refusal) is `VisualHost::notice`, shown as legacy's modal message box titled "Warning" (VisualClips.cpp:1013-1016) until OK; legacy's wxBell is `VisualHost::bell`.

For T5:

## The Perspective tool (VT3)

**The maths.** libass renders a Line's box (`Box`, measured at a scale of 100 through `TextMeasurePort`, or a drawing's points at its \p scale, read as libass's drawing_tokenize reads them: nothing before the first m with a point, no ink at all after an n before any m, a b's points in threes, an s's three or none, a p's once there are three) sheared by \fax about its top, scaled about the alignment point at \pos, turned about \org (Z, then X, then Y) and projected from `screenZ` in front of the screen: 20000 / 64 layout pixels, which in script units is 312.5 × PlayResY / layout height (LayoutResY, else the video's frame height, else PlayResY). `quadToTransform` inverts it: the plane's normal gives \fry and \frx, the top edge \frz, the shear between the edges \fax and their lengths \fscx and \fscy; \xbord, \ybord, \xshad and \yshad scale with them, so the outline keeps its proportion. The \org mode chooses \org: Center (the quad's centre), No \fax (where the shear is zero, for Lines with line breaks) or Keep (where it is; its handle can be dragged and its fields typed). arch1t3cht's No \fax took the wrong root when the circle's radius was zero; the rewrite takes the circle's centre there.

**Reading the Line** follows libass (`ass_parse.c`): spaces and tabs may come between a backslash and its tag's name; arguments are split at commas, each without the spaces and tabs around it, and an empty one is left out (\pos(100,) has one value); \pos and \org take two values and \move four or six (a word is 0), else the tag is skipped; a \move with neither time above 0 runs over the Line's span; the first \an or \a decides (out of range, the Style's), \a4 and \a8 being \a5; \fs+n and \fs-n scale the size by 1 ± n / 10 (only with the sign first: \fs +10 is 10), and \fs0 goes back to the Style's; \fsc sets both scales back to the current Style's; \fscx, \fscy, the outlines and \shad are held at 0 and above (\xshad and \yshad are not), as are a Style's scales, spacing, outline and shadow, so a negative scale collapses the text and it has no quad; a \r's Style and a \fn's font are named without the spaces after them (\fn0 is the Style's font); \n breaks a line only under wrap style 2 (WrapStyle, or the last \q before it), \h is a hard space, a tab is a space, \{ and \} show braces, and a { with no } after it is text. A Line that libass places by collisions (neither \pos, \move, \org nor any \t, and no scrolling effect) is stacked clear of the Lines shown with it as libass places it (`ass_render.c` finish_frame, fix_collisions and fit_rect, on a track without placement history), not by the rule the other tools stack it by (GetDialogueAdditionalPosition), which sums the heights of the Lines meeting its own unmoved rectangle: each earlier obstacle is placed in its turn clear of those placed before it, and the Line last, a bottom-aligned one moving up and another down. Only earlier Lines of its Layer that collide count (a Comment is not rendered), each a rectangle of its box at its own leading \fscx and \fscy with its outline (its tags', else its Style's) and without its shadow, as libass's event rectangle; an obstacle whose border changes after its text (\bord, \xbord or \ybord: libass's rectangle takes the largest) cannot be measured; a Line with nothing shown (spaces alone, which libass drops at either end of a row, a tab and a soft \n being spaces and \h not) is no obstacle, nor counts towards the 256 obstacles past which the stack is off the frame. libass reads no `Collisions:` field, so neither does the tool: Reverse stacks as Normal, as the video shows it. When one in its band cannot be measured the tool gives no quad, since where libass puts the Line is not known; past 256 Lines in its band the stack is off the frame and is not measured further. A Line with a scrolling effect (Banner;, or Scroll up; or Scroll down; with their three values) moves with the time and has no quad. The tool gives no quad to a drawing with text or another drawing after it, to text with a drawing in it, to text where a tag changes the glyphs after some of it (\fs, \fn, \fscx, \fscy, \fsp, \b, \i, an angle, a shear or \r), to a Line with a \t holding any tag but a colour, an alpha, a blur or a clip (libass interpolates the animatable ones with the time and runs any other, a \pos or an \an say, at once), to text at a weight other than normal and bold (\b500: libass renders the weight, the measure has the two), to a vertical font (@name), to a font the renderer alone is given (one of the Script's attachments or the external fonts, by any of the family, full or PostScript names in its bytes, of which at most 256 faces, 16384 name records and 1 MiB of names are read, ignoring ASCII case as libass; the measure would take another in its place), to text with a character its font lacks while any font is leased (libass may take it from a leased font, the measure from one of the system's), to text with a character outside the BMP under a \fsp (the measure spaces each UTF-16 unit, as legacy's text_extents does, libass each character), or to text wider than the room between its margins under a wrapping style (libass wraps it into rows; the margins are the Line's, else its own Style's, which a \r does not change): one box at one Style's size does not hold those. Nor does it give one when the Line and the Lines it stacks on come to more than 512 KiB of text (the translation's in translation mode, which is what is read), 64 rows, 4096 shown characters or 256 Ki code units of drawing together, and an edit whose targets come to more than 512 KiB of text is refused: each row is measured through the font on the UI thread, so a hostile Line (a long comment of \N, say), or many Lines shown at once, cost no more than that in one read. The leading \fsp, \b and \i go into the measure; a tag without the value it takes resets as libass does (\frx, \fry, \fax and \fay to 0, \frz, \fscx, \fscy, the outline and the shadow to the current Style's). A drawing's \pbo (the last before it, which \r leaves, at its \p scale) goes into its box as libass lays it out: the ascent is the height less \pbo and the descent is \pbo, neither below 0 on the line, so an offset past the height moves the ink down and one outside 0 to the height makes the line the alignment uses taller than the ink. A Line with \org takes no part in the stacking, as libass leaves it out of collisions. A Line's Style is found as libass's ass_lookup_style finds it: without leading *s, "default" in any case being Default, the last Style of the name, else the last Default; with neither the Line is on libass's own Default (Arial 18 at weight 200, outline 2, shadow 3, margins 20; a \rDefault then resets to it too), whose text has no quad unless a \b sets a weight the measure takes, while a drawing on it has one. An outline or shadow tag after the text gives the edited Line no quad, and a change of scale is refused while another picked Line has one: the tool scales them in the leading blocks only. Where the video's time makes a Line unreadable (an obstacle it cannot measure comes on screen, say) its quad goes until it can be read again. The Styles are decoded and indexed by name once per revision of the Script (`StyleCache`; a \r takes the last of its name, as libass), so reading the Line again each frame costs no decoding, and many Styles and many resets take linear time. Each line is read in one pass, so a hostile line takes linear time. A typed angle or shear drops the Line's \fay, which the tool does not write, as a corner's edit does.

**Gestures** (owner-approved on the card). A corner drag moves that corner; Shift locks it to the nearer of the plane's two axes; Ctrl snaps it to another handle's x or y within 10 units (the vector editor's FindSnapPoint rule); Alt scales from the centre, the opposite corner mirroring it. A drag inside the inner quad moves it (within the plane while the plane is shown and unlocked); a drag inside the outer quad alone moves both. A double click puts the leading quad's nearest corner at the pointer. The arrows nudge the corner grabbed last, or the whole quad before one is, one unit (Shift a tenth), committing on the release. A drag that would fold a quad is not taken. Every drag, double click or nudge is one "Visual perspective tool" step over every picked Line: each gets the active Line's tags, its own outline and shadow scaled by the same ratio; a Line with \move keeps its motion, both points moved by the change in position, and its quad is where the video's time puts it, following playback and seeks. A click alone records nothing, and an arrow step that would fold the quad changes no Line. libass turns a Line with \move and no \org of its own about its moving position, which a fixed \org cannot hold: on such a Line a drag finds the turn about its position (whatever the \org mode) and no \org is written, nor one when the tags do not turn it; a turn of other picked Lines that move without an \org of their own is refused, as is any turn that cannot keep it. An \org typed or dragged in Keep is written as given.

**The plane.** Show surrounding plane adds the outer quad, a rectangle of the same plane twice the inner one's size at first: matched to a rectangle in the shot, it holds the perspective while the inner quad is placed in it (an inner corner moves within the plane, the tags following). Lock outer quad (greyed while the plane is hidden) swaps the roles: the outer quad is placed by itself and an inner corner sets the perspective. Show grid draws an 8 × 8 grid over the outer quad (4 × 4 over the inner one without it). The outer quad is saved with each Line it edits, in Aegisub's extradata as arch1t3cht's Aegisub saves it (`_aegi_perspective_ambient_plane`, XD1; see document-model.md), in the same history step as the tags. A locked resize writes no tag but changes that value, so it is a step of its own. Undo brings back the value the Line had, and a file saved by either editor opens with its planes in place. Hiding the plane leaves the value as it was. A Line neither moving nor stacked is read again only when the script changes, not every frame. A plane shown when its Line cannot be read for a while (a stacking obstacle that cannot be measured) comes back where it was once the Line can be. Shown, it stays put while a \move carries the inner quad, and when the Line is read again after an edit, an Undo or a new Style; showing it fixes it from then on. Hidden, it stays where it was: shown again after the inner quad moved, the inner quad is placed in it as arch1t3cht does (its first and third corners in the plane's coordinates). It is shown only where it holds (`planeHolds`: the outer quad finite, convex and short of the plane's horizon, where the projective map's divisor reaches 0): a steeply turned quad's plane starts smaller than twice it, down to the inner quad itself, and an edit that would take the plane past its horizon is not taken.

**Perspective tracking.** FA13 ([#570](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/issues/570), approved 2026-11-04) ports arch1t3cht's Perspective-Motion 0.4.1 (MIT) as Video › Tracking › Perspective tracking… (GLOBAL_PERSPECTIVE_TRACKING, 5240, no default key; greyed without a video). It reads pasted After Effects CC Power Pin data (`parsePowerPin`, perspective_tracking.h), whose length must equal the frames the selection covers. Lines that overlap must all show at the relative frame. Apply splits each Line frame by frame (`lineFrames`, grid_split.h) and maps each part's quad from the relative frame's plane to that frame's (`toUV`/`fromUV`, `quadToTransform`), with optional "Fit flat lines to the plane first" (the script's Apply perspective), clips moved with the plane (`mapClip`) and outline and shadow scaled. The frame's quad is always saved as the plane. It is one history step, "Perspective tracking", and the new Lines are selected. Data tracked at another size is scaled to the video, with a warning in the dialog.

**Motion data.** AU8 ([#842](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/issues/842), approved 2026-11-09) does natively what Aegisub-Motion, 0x.DeriveTrack, arch.DerivePerspTrack, petzku.ExtrapolateMove and phos.ExtrapolateTracking do, in Video › Tracking (5257–5262, no default keys). The bundled Aegisub-Motion still ships unchanged. Its departures are [AU8-motion-data](compatibility-decisions.md#au8-motion-data). The parity table and corpus are in `docs/research/motion-data-parity.md`.

- **Apply motion data…** (`motion_data.h` `applyMotion`) reads pasted or opened After Effects 6.0 keyframe data (Position, Scale, Rotation) and does Aegisub-Motion's per-frame maths (`motion_tags.cpp`). Each selected Line becomes one Line a frame, or one Line with `\move` and `\t` in Linear mode, in one undo step. The new Lines keep Aegisub-Motion's `a-mo` extradata, so either Revert undoes either Apply. **Track clips with other data** adds a second data box; the clips follow it from the same relative frame. **Extrapolate past the data** fits data shorter than the Lines past its ends (`extendTrack`), and the status line warns when a fit is poor.
- **Revert motion data** puts each tracked Line back as the Line it was made from, spanning all its pieces.
- **Copy motion data from lines** writes the selected frame-by-frame Lines' first-block `\pos` (or the `\move` point), `\fscx`, `\fscy` and `\frz` as keyframe data at the video's frame rate. Applying it again to the first Line gives back the same Lines.
- **Copy Power-Pin track from lines** (`derivePowerPinTrack`) writes each Line's saved plane, else its tags' quad, in arch.DerivePerspTrack's layout. A frame no Line shows keeps the quad before it. FA13's `parsePowerPin` reads it back.
- **Extend tracked lines…** fits each value of frame-by-frame Lines with phos's method (`motion_fit.h`: degree 1 to 3 by k-fold cross-validation, the window halved towards the extended end while the error is 0.003 or more). It then grows the first or last Line by N frames and applies the fit as motion data.
- **Extrapolate \move to the line** carries each selected Line's timed `\move` on at the same speed to its whole span, written to two decimals.

**The bar.** Plane (the three toggles, K1 roles perspective-plane, perspective-lock-outer and perspective-grid), Origin (the mode as a text dropdown, then X and Y, editable in Keep), Rotation (X, Y and Z, °) and Shear (\fax). Values in the group of the option before them (Origin's X and Y) join that group as one item of the bar's Flow, under its label, so a wrap never parts them from it (`VisualToolBar.qml`'s `joined`).

**Drawing.** The inner quad in red at two pixels, the outer quad dashed, the grid faint, the corners' squares (inner) and circles (outer), and \org's orange cross (with a circle in Keep), in device pixels as the other tools' handles.

The render check is `hikari_backends_visual_perspective_render_tests`: libass renders the tool's tags and the ink's corners must lie within a pixel of the quad, at the script's size and at twice it.
## The Gradient tool (AU7)

AU7 ([#843](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/issues/843), owner gate Q227–Q229 on 2026-11-10) adds a thirteenth family, `GradientTool` (`visual_gradient.h`), last on the rail. Its command is VIDEO_TOOL_GRADIENT (2043, W). It does natively what Gradient Factory, Skew gradient, lyger's GradientEverything, GradientByChar and ClipGrad, phos.VectorGradient and RotateGradient, zf.gradientCut, petzku's CombineGradientLines, ua.Colorize and l0.LerpByChar do. The bundled scripts still ship unchanged; their defects are [#885](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/issues/885). Departures are [AU7-gradient](compatibility-decisions.md#au7-gradient).

The UI-free parts are shared with AU22 and NE5:

- `tag_lerp.h`: tag values (number, colour, alpha, point, rect), RGB or HSL blending, Style defaults, ASS formatting, stops and Accel (t^a).
- `gradient_geometry.h`: polygons in script pixels, filled nonzero as libass fills clips. Sutherland–Hodgman against strips (always convex), `positiveRegion` to rebuild a self-intersecting or self-touching outline, `inset` and `insetBands` for strips along a clip (round joins at reflex corners), drawings in and out, and the five phos masks.
- `gradient.h`: a `Spec` and the target Lines in, the new texts out, or a `Problem` saying why not.

**Modes** (icon toggles): Rectangle strips (snapped to the drag's dominant axis, `\clip(x1,y1,x2,y2)`), Rotated strips, Skewed strips (a handle tilts the strip edges; a 4-point vector clip seeds it), Strips along the clip (rings inward from the Line's clip, no drag), Wedge strips (P1–P3, or the Line's 3-point vector clip; Shape: Wedge, Box, Ring, Star or Vignette), By character (a block before each grapheme, t by measured width or by count) and By line (each picked Line by its index).

**Stops.** Values: From and To for each tag in the gradient (the picker adds a tag, Remove drops the shown one), From seeded from the active Line. Picked lines (strip modes): every picked Line is a stop in Grid order, and tags that differ between them are interpolated block by block. Strip, Overlap, Accel, Blend and Merge equal strips (on by default) shape the strips.

**Preview and commit.** After the drag (at once in the modes without one) the new Lines show over the video through `previewLines`, and `hiddenLines` leaves the originals out of the preview. Handles, values and options still change it. Apply or Enter commits one "Gradient" step through `Gesture::stageReplacement`: each target is commented and its strips follow it (the stops are all commented, and the strips follow the last), or its text is changed in the other modes. The new Lines are selected. Esc drops the preview. A gradient that can't be made says why over the video, and Apply rings the bell.

## Blur and glow layers (AU18)

AU18 ([#852](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/issues/852), owner gate Q240 on 2026-11-11) builds natively what ua.BlurAndGlow, lyger.BorderSplit and NecrosCopy's 3D shadow build: layered copies of each selected Dialogue Line. None of the three is bundled, so there is no byte-identical target. The window is `BlurAndGlowLayersDialog.qml`, which is modeless. It opens from Subtitles > "Blur and glow layers…" (5264), the Grid row menu and the Line editor's text menu. 5265 builds again with the values last built with, which are kept in `typesetting.layerOptions`.

- `layer_builders.h`: `buildLayers` gives one Line's stack. `planLayers` covers the whole selection: the new layers, the skipped Lines, the Lines a stack will cover and the limits. `applyLayerPlan` writes it, and `buildLayerStacks` is the one "Blur and glow layers" step. Tags are read and written with `override_blocks.h`, frame_bake's parser.
- **Order**, bottom to top: glows in list order, shadow copies farthest first, the second border, the border, then the source. A stack of k Lines takes layers L … L+k−1 and the source moves to L+k. The source keeps its LineId, so its extradata and QC notes stay with it. The built Lines go in just above it in the Grid and carry no extradata. Other Lines' layers never change.
- **Glow**: every `\blur` takes the glow's blur and the border grows by its Border. `\alpha` and `\3a` rise to its Alpha (`\1a` on a Line without a border), and a colour replaces `\3c` (`\c`). The glow keeps the Line's shadow (Q4) unless Shadow is on, because then the copies are the shadow.
- **Border**: with Border colour, the border layer's fill is its border (`\c` after each `\3c`, `\1a` after each `\3a`, the Style's after `\r`) and the top gets `\bord0`. With Transparent, the border layer hides its fill and grows by the blur, and the top keeps a fill-coloured border as wide as the blur. Border blur replaces the border layer's `\blur`. During `\fad` the border layer hides its fill (`\1a&HFF&` with `\t` over the fade transition). The second border is the border layer widened, in its own colour (the fill by default), and keeps the shadow.
- **Shadow**: the steps default to one a pixel of the larger shadow offset. Copy k of N has `\xshad`/`\yshad` × k/N (cut to 2 decimals) and `\1a&HFE&\3a&HFF&`, with `\4c`/`\4a` ramped from the nearest copy to the farthest. The ramp is new. The top drops its shadow.
- `\clip`, `\move`, `\pos`, `\org`, `\fad`, drawings and the text stay on every layer. Inside `\t`, only what a layer changes is mapped. A Line without a border skips Border, a Line whose shadow is under 1 px skips Shadow, and the status line counts both. Built layers get "Blur where a line has none" (0.6) when a Line has no `\blur` or `\be`.
- **Limits**: 8 glows, 64 shadow steps and 20 000 built Lines a run.
- **Preview**: while the window is open, the video shows the selected Lines' stacks through `showVideoSubtitles` (the Template editor's document swap). It follows the options and the selection 100 ms after a change, and closing the window drops it.

### What the scripts write (read, not run)

Sources: `ua.BlurAndGlow.lua` 2.5 (corpus copy, unanimated-Aegisub-Scripts@ade40a212b0a), `lyger.BorderSplit.lua` 1.1.0 (TypesettingTools feed, sha1 caa12243177b44e2e92e61df45029337474606f6), `ua.NecrosCopy.lua` 4.1 (unanimated.github.io/ts/necros.lua; function `shad3`). L = the source Line's layer, i = its row.

#### ua.BlurAndGlow

Every built Line first gets `preprocess`: `\blur<def>` (default 0.6) at the start of the first block when the Line has no `\blur`, and after each `\r`; `\1c` becomes `\c`; a first block with `\t` but no `\3c` before it gets the Style's `\3c`.

| Button / case | Rows after the macro (top row first) | Layers | What each row's text gets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blur / Layers, one border | row i: border, row i+1: top | L, L+1 | **border** (`borderline`): `\c` removed, then `\c` = the `\3c` (Style's 3c when the first block has none; also after each `\r` from that Style), every `\3c&H..&` followed by the same `\c`; "bottom blur" replaces every `\blur` value. **top** (`topline`): `\bord`/`\shad` dropped from `\t`, empty `\t` removed, `\bord0` (every `\bord` → `\bord0`, one at the first block's start if none, after each `\r`), `\xbord`/`\ybord` and `\3c` removed, `\shad<shadow>` + `\4a&HFF&` in the first block (and after `\r`), duplicates collapsed. "keep shadow on top layer" drops the `\4a&HFF&`. |
| Blur / Layers, double border | 2nd border, 1st border, top | L, L+1, L+2 | **2nd border** (`borderline2`): `\bord` = 2 × border (or border + "2nd b. size"), `\xbord`/`\ybord` doubled, `\3c` = the fill colour (or "2nd b. colour"), `\c` kept. **1st border** as above plus `\4a&HFF&` unless "keep shadow on middle layer". |
| Blur + Glow, one border | glow, border, top | L, L+1, L+2 | **glow** (`glowlayer`): every `\blur` value → glow blur; `\alpha` and `\3a` raised to at least glow alpha (default 80), `\alpha&H<a>&` added to the first block when it has none (left out when alpha is 00); glow colour, if set, replaces every `\3c` and goes in the first block. Fill and shadow are left as they are. |
| Blur + Glow, double border | glow, 2nd border, 1st border, top | L … L+3 | the glow is built from the 2nd-border text. |
| Blur + Glow, no border | glow, top (unchanged) | L, L+1 | the glow uses `\c` / `\1a` instead of `\3c` / `\3a`. |
| only add glow | glow, the Line unchanged | L, L+1 | |
| fix `\1a` with fade (default on, 45 ms) | — | — | on the border (and 2nd-border) rows of a Line with `\fad(in,out)`: `\1a&HFF&\t(in−T,in,\1a&H00&)` and `\t(dur−out,dur−out+T,\1a&HFF&)` in the first block. |

The selection stays on the top rows.

#### lyger.BorderSplit ("Duplicate and Blur")

Runs on every Line whose Actor starts `bord` (or `shad`), not on the selection. Modes `s` (default) and `a`; options `g`, `d`. The original is kept as a comment (Actor gets a leading `*`), the new rows are inserted after it with Effect `done`.

| Mode | Rows (top row first) | Layers | Text |
|---|---|---|---|
| `s` | comment, border, top | —, L, L+1 | **top**: `\3c`, `\3a`, `\shad`, `\xshad`, `\yshad` removed; every `\bord` outside `\t` → `\bord0` (plus `\blur0.6` if the Line had no blur); `\bord0` at the head if no `\bord`; `\shad0` at the head when the Style has a shadow; empty `\t()` removed. **border**: `\c`, `\1c`, `\1a` removed; `\1c` = `\3c` and `\1a` = `\3a` (head and alongside each); `\blur0.6` at the head if none. |
| `a` | comment, border, top | —, L, L+1 | **top**: as `s`, but `\3c` = `\1c`, `\3a` = `\1a` and `\bord` = the blur value (a thin border of fill colour). **border**: `\bord` + blur, every `\alpha` → `\3a`, `\1a&HFF&` at the head. |
| `d` option | comment, 3rd, border, top | —, L, L+1, L+2 | **3rd**: `\3c` = `\1c`; `s`: `\bord` × 2; `a`: `\bord` × 2 + blur and `\1a&HFF&`. The middle row loses its shadow. |

#### ua.NecrosCopy "3D shadow" (`shad3`)

- With no shadow tag, `\shad<Style's shadow>` is put at the start; `\shad<n>` in the first block becomes `\xshad<n>\yshad<n>`; duplicate tags in the first block are collapsed.
- N = floor(max(|xshad|, |yshad|)) of the first block (outside `\t`). With N < 1 the Line is left as it is.
- It writes N rows in place of the Line, top row first: the original text with `\xshad`/`\yshad` × 1/N at layer L+N−1, then copies with `\xshad`/`\yshad` × k/N (k = 2 … N, values cut to 2 decimals) at layers L+N−k, each with `\3a&HFF&` and `\1a&HFE&` in the first block (fill nearly and border fully transparent, so only the shadow shows). There is no colour or alpha ramp. The original row is deleted.

## Interpolate across Lines (AU22)

AU22 ([#856](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/issues/856), owner gate Q247–Q250 on 2026-11-11) does natively what lyger.FbfTransform and line0's Cascade Transforms do. The command is GLOBAL_INTERPOLATE_LINES (5267, no default key): Grid context menu › "Interpolate across lines…", after Split, greyed below three selected Lines. Neither script is bundled. The departures are [AU22-interpolate](compatibility-decisions.md#au22-interpolate). The behaviour record is on the card.

- `tag_lerp.h` holds the maths shared with the Gradient tool, including vector clip drawings (`Drawing`: the same commands and point counts lerp point by point).
- `interpolate.h`: `plan` takes the run's Lines and the options and returns the tags that change, and why any can't, plus every Line's new text. `selectedRun` reads the shown selected Lines in Grid order. `applyPlan` writes them as one "Interpolate across Lines" step. `cascadeTransforms` is line0's re-timing.

**What it changes.** The first and last selected Lines are the ends; every Line between them gets values by its place in the run, t = (i/(n−1))^Acceleration. Only tags whose value differs between the ends are listed and written: numbers, colours and alphas per channel, `\pos`, `\org`, rect and vector `\clip`/`\iclip`, and `\t` lists of the same shape (their times, acceleration and values). Inline blocks line up by character offset in the text without tags. When the ends' texts differ, only the start block counts and the dialog says so. A tag one end lacks takes the value of an earlier block, else the Style's; `\pos` falls back to `\move`'s start, then the alignment position. In each middle Line the tag is removed from its blocks and their `\t`, then the new value is written; a value the previous block already has is not repeated. Other tags and the text are left alone.

**The dialog** (`InterpolateLinesWindow.qml`, not modal). The tags table (tick to include; a clip or `\t` that can't be interpolated is greyed with the reason), Acceleration, Rotate the shortest way, Cascade transforms (each Line's `\t` run back to back over the Line), and the Result list of new texts. While it is open the video shows the result (`Application::interpolationPlan` puts the texts over the Lines in `showVideoSubtitles`), and a change of selection plans again. Apply or Enter commits one step; Esc or Cancel changes nothing.

## Edit shapes and Edit tags (PU5)
PU5 ([#307](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/issues/307), [mockup](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/issues/307#issuecomment-6336250092) approved by the owner 2026-10-22) puts both editors on DG1's frame as modeless, resizable windows (`ShapesEditionDialog.qml`, `AllTagsEditionDialog.qml`, kept where they were left under `shapesEdition` and `allTagsEdition`), one layout for both: the list on the left with New, Duplicate, Delete and ⋯ under it, the selected item's fields on the right, its preview, and a footer with Undo and Redo at the leading edge and Close at the trailing one.
A click or Up and Down in either list chooses through the editor; the list's highlight follows the editor's selection only. The bar's chosen preset or tag follows a rename (the name typed, Replace, their Undo and Redo, Undo all: the history keeps each step's renames, and Redo brings back the step's own selection), and a change made while another family is shown reaches the drawing tool or Hydra all the same, so it comes back on the same entry, read again. New shape, Take from the active line and the tags preview read the Line editor's pending text, as the video shows it. A window that goes with the one it opened from (fullscreen left, a floating panel hidden) is not closed: the editor and its history stay, the bar showing the dialogs opens it again, and Edit shapes… or Edit tags… brings an open editor's window forward.
**Kept as it is made.** Every change goes back to the tool and into the file at once (`ShapeEditor`'s and `AllTagsEditor`'s `hooks.changed`; the controller's `saveShapes` and `saveAllTags`). The window's history (`EditHistory`, whole-list snapshots) makes each change one step, the typing in one field of one item one step; Undo and Redo (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Shift+Z; a text field's own undo comes first while it has the keyboard) walk it and name the step in their tooltips. Close and Esc keep everything (PU5-apply-as-you-go). ⋯ holds Restore default shapes / tags, which asks first (PK1's message box, the warning, "Restore defaults" destructive, Cancel the default) and is one step, and Undo all changes, one step back to the list as the window opened.
**Refusals.** A value the tool cannot use is said under its field in the danger colour with the error icon (`FieldError.qml`) and the item keeps its last good value; leaving the field shows that value again (PU5-inline-errors). Names: empty, a separator (";" for shapes, "," for tags), or another item's name ignoring case; the last asks on Enter or on leaving the field "Replace the shape / tag “…”?" with Replace (destructive) and Keep editing (the default), and Replace is one step (PU5-name-clash). A shape's drawing: empty or with a ";". A tag's numbers: legacy's NumCtrl reading (a comma as the point), within ±10000, decimal places 0–6, and legacy Save's range checks said for the field typed: the maximum above the minimum, the step above zero and at most half the range.
**Edit shapes.** The list draws each preset small (`ShapeDrawing.qml`, the drawing's `ShapeEditor::svgPath`) and marks the user's own. New shape takes the active Line's drawing, else a 100 × 100 square; Duplicate copies the preset as "… copy"; Delete asks nothing and is off on the last preset. "Scale from the cursor" is legacy's preset mode as three buttons (Width and height, Keep aspect ratio, Width only); the scaling mode a file can set is kept and not shown (PU5-scaling-hidden). Take from the active line replaces the drawing with the Line's last one. A change reaches the drawing tool at once, the chosen preset included (legacy's SetShape kept the one it had at the same place), and the bar's choice follows its preset's name when one before it goes or comes back. The list and the preview draw a B-spline (`s`, `p`) as the renderer does, not its control points. The preview draws the shape around the click's cross on a 50-unit grid with its size.
**Edit tags.** The list is the Hydra strip's: Common, More (folded, with its count) and Your tags, each tag beside its name, with a search over names and tags (Enter takes the first match). A definition shows its name, its tag (typed without its backslash) and its default, the starting values named as the strip names them, first. Behind "Advanced" (folded at each opening; HY2 [#272](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/issues/272), folded into PU5, PU5-tag-advanced) are the range, step and decimal places, Count 1–4 for legacy's "No / One / Two / Three additional values", and "Starts on": the strip's Set / Add / Multiply / Gradient and the choice beside it, legacy's eight change options and Set's three placings, stored as before. The preview shows the strip as Hydra shows the tag and what Set writes at the starting values on the active Line (the first Line when none is active), following another Line chosen while the window is open. A new tag reaches the strip once it has a tag, and Delete is off on the last definition with a tag while new ones wait for theirs; a new one named as that definition says to fill in Tag first rather than offering to replace it. A range refused for its step names half the range as typed. A pasted tag loses the blanks around it and its leading backslashes. The strip's placing choice goes through an open Edit tags as one of its steps; the strip's sections are `allTagsSections`, the window's.
