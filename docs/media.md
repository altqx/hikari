# Media transport, presentation and editor audio

**Accepted pipeline proposal, 2026-09-27.** [Altqx approved the current proposal](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/issues/25#issuecomment-5853349062), including FFMS2 indexed editing, Qt Multimedia as the first general-player implementation to qualify, the CPU/libass reference followed by scene-graph presentation, and optional Windows adapters. In the live review, altqx accepted general-player ownership of audio and clock while that mode is active, and **PortAudio editor output with QSG waveform geometry and cached spectrum tiles**. [ADR 0009](adr/0009-active-mode-media-transport.md) and [ADR 0010](adr/0010-portaudio-editor-output.md) record the choices for [Choose the video decode, playback and presentation pipeline](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/issues/25) and [Choose the audio output backend and waveform renderer](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/issues/26). They select responsibilities and implementation direction; they do not certify a native pipeline, timing accuracy or complete player parity.

## Active mode owns sound and playback time

HikariSub's application transport coordinates two modes behind explicit backend ports:

| Mode | Decode/output direction | Clock responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Indexed editing | FFMS2 supplies indexed video and decoded audio; PortAudio is the sole editor-output library. | An audio-derived estimate drives editor playback scheduling and the audio cursor. Intentionally silent playback uses an explicit monotonic-clock mode. |
| General playback | A cross-platform player handles unindexed playback, its audio device and A/V scheduling. Qt Multimedia's FFmpeg-backed player is the **first candidate to verify**. | The active player owns its clock; editor PortAudio output is stopped. |

Only the active mode owns playback audio and timing. This does not require routing the general player's PCM through PortAudio. Do not add Qt/SDL/another library as a silent editor-output fallback. A candidate player's missing capability must be surfaced and resolved through its adapter or an explicit revised choice.

For a mode switch, stop and flush the old transport, invalidate its generation and clock estimates, map the media identity and requested position, and acknowledge the destination presentation before resuming. Indexed destinations acknowledge the requested frame identity; a general player's acknowledgement must describe its actual position/accuracy, without pretending a time seek is an exact indexed step. Device-buffer tails and handoff latency need measurement. Handoff does not rewrite Document timing.

Clock ownership and the [accepted time contract](time-semantics.md) are separate responsibilities. Backends implement its canonical units, interval boundaries and preserved rounding rules, including the named approved departures; renderer timestamps and global FPS cannot become authoritative Document state. Native time conversion and acknowledgement evidence remains required.

## Video, subtitles and visual tools

FFMS2 remains required for indexed editing and libass for subtitle rendering. General playback remains a capability distinct from indexing; the selected player must cover existing track, chapter, embedded-subtitle and fullscreen workflows. The inspected Qt player offers time seeking and track selection but does not establish exact indexed stepping or the required chapter-list API. Qt Multimedia is therefore a candidate, with explicit native parity work still required. [Playback research](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/blob/4c90a86bead00528e5636f012e690c618e803f4f/docs/research/general-playback.md).

Begin with owned CPU frames and a **CPU-BGRA/libass reference**, then implement and verify a **Qt Quick scene-graph texture/material presenter**, keeping subtitle overlays and visual-tool handles separate. The production item/API, pixel upload path and any native-texture optimization remain evidence-driven implementation work. No application-wide OpenGL requirement follows from the existing renderer or an example integration. [Presentation research](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/blob/26d7ac2a940c9f0edf2c6e65f5b1db05ea1a2507/docs/research/qtquick-video.md).

| Port or owner | Required responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Video source | Open/index with progress and cancellation; identify tracks; return owned immutable frames with generation, frame identity, source timestamp/timebase, geometry/SAR/crop, planes/strides and color metadata. Distinguish unsupported operations from failures. |
| Transport | Own active mode, seeks/steps, bounded queues and clock validity. Playback may drop late frames; exact paused requests return the requested indexed frame or an error, not an unannounced later frame. FFMS2 seek settings must substantiate that promise. |
| Subtitle renderer | Render a Document/font/configuration snapshot at an explicit time and geometry. Copy library-owned output before expiry; preserve blend order/alpha. Redraw paused subtitles independently from video decode and prevent duplicate player subtitle rendering. |
| Presenter | Own graphics resources/upload/composition on Qt's render thread. Never wait for decoding there; retain frame data until use finishes. Distinguish logical coordinates from physical pixels. |
| Visual tools | Keep command state and overlay geometry independent of decoded textures. Share source/script-to-view transforms and inverse hit testing, with numeric/keyboard alternatives and useful behavior during dummy video or decoder failure. |

Keep range, matrix, primaries and transfer metadata distinct; honor the existing ASS matrix override and record missing-metadata fallback. Establish SDR CPU/GPU and subtitle-alpha parity as the first reference. This is neither an HDR correctness claim nor approval to remove HDR-related capability. The [overlay research](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/blob/fb510f491048b3a073ace4076cf12bc949345c53/docs/research/visual-overlays.md) supports separate tool presentation.

### Playback pipeline

While playing, the GUI thread only shows results. A player hands each frame to the session's `PlaybackPipeline` from its own thread (Qt's video sink thread, DirectShow's streaming thread). Only the newest frame waits for the worker, and only the newest result waits for the GUI thread; anything older is dropped and counted, so a slow moment never leaves a backlog to play through. The worker converts the frame to BGRA into pooled buffers, renders the subtitle overlay at the frame's start, and renders the next frame's overlay ahead while it waits. Results carry the subtitles serial they were rendered for: a result made before a new prepare is rendered again on the GUI thread, and one from before a pause, stop or seek is dropped. The renderer selection serializes `select` against `prepare` and `render` so the worker may render while the GUI thread prepares.

- **Conversion.** `videoFrameToBgra` converts NV12, NV21, P010, P016, YUV420P, YV12, YUV420P10 and YUV422P itself, and anything else through `QVideoFrame::toImage`. It uses the matrix and range the indexed video is converted with at the time (`VideoSession::playerColour`: V4's legacy YCbCr matrix, so a "TV.601" script shows a BT.709 source as BT.601 while playing too), so a player frame looks like the paused frame at the same time, as legacy's playback through the same FFMS2 provider did. Against the paused frame the mean difference is under one level per channel for both a TV.709 and a TV.601 script; the rest is chroma upsampling (FFMS2's swscale interpolates, this repeats). Before, Qt's conversion followed the stream's own tags with a graphics device and BT.601 without one. Like the indexed frames there is no transfer conversion (BT.2020 PQ or HLG is not tone-mapped) and no rotation or mirroring: the paused frames, the subtitles' frame size and legacy all take the coded picture as it is, where `toImage` applied a stream's rotation. A hardware-decoded frame is read back by `QVideoFrame::map` on the worker; FFmpeg's transfer serializes on the device's own lock. Measured with CUDA (P010 from HEVC Main 10) on Linux. Windows D3D11VA is not exercised because the VM has no hardware decoder. DirectShow samples use legacy's own coefficients, bit for bit. When the presenter converts on the GPU (below), `videoFrameToYcbcr` copies the same formats into 8-bit Y'CbCr planes instead (`IndexedFrame::ycbcr`: luma, then CbCr pairs or separate Cb and Cr; 16-bit samples rounded as the CPU path rounds them, NV21 and YV12 swapped, a short chroma mapping's last row repeated), with the same matrix and range; other formats still become BGRA through `toImage`. DirectShow's YV12 and NV12 samples become planes the same way (RGB32 and YUY2 stay BGRA). A 1080p frame is 3.1 MB of planes where BGRA takes 8.3 MB.
- **Rendering ahead.** Every renderer port draws a time to the millisecond (`subtitle_render.h`), so the worker renders the next frame's overlay once, at the millisecond it guesses that frame starts in, and keys it by that millisecond. The guess is one frame on at the constant pace the starts so far allow. A Matroska start is rounded to the millisecond, so the worker keeps the polygon of (start, interval) pairs every rounded start allows and narrows it frame by frame. On a 23.976 fps millisecond timebase about 98% of the guesses hit. While half or more of the last 16 guesses missed (a variable frame rate) nothing is rendered ahead. Counters report what was planned, rendered, used and left unused.
- **Presenter.** The presenter keeps persistent textures on the RHI backends and updates them in place: a frame shown again is not uploaded, and an overlay uploads only its visible rectangle together with the previous one's (to clear it). The software renderer makes an image of the visible part only. An overlay with nothing visible is not drawn. A texture the backend cannot make, for example one larger than its largest texture, fails that present with "texture upload failed" instead of reporting a frame it never drew. On an RHI backend with R8 and RG8 textures (checked: OpenGL on Linux, Direct3D 11 on Windows) the presenter converts frames kept as planes in a fragment shader (`src/ui/shaders/ycbcr.frag`) with the CPU conversion's fixed-point formula, so each pixel is the byte `ycbcrToBgra` makes on the CPU (tested within one level, from float rounding). The frame is uploaded as planes once and an overlay texture is composited over it by the scene graph as before. Both frame layers sample the nearest texel. The software renderer and an RHI backend without those formats get BGRA frames: the frame worker asks the presenter (`PresenterPort::convertsYcbcr`) before converting, and a planar frame that reaches a presenter without the shader is converted on the CPU (`ycbcrToBgra`) and counted. Upload counters (`VideoPresenter::uploadStats`) report the planar uploads, the CPU conversions and the last frame's upload bytes.
- **Notifications.** The session notifies once per player frame. The Video panel's texts (time, status) and its seek bar follow the player's frame (the indexed frame holding the last delivered frame's start, legacy GetCurrentFrame) at most every 100 ms while playing, as legacy's VideoBox time timer refreshed the time and the slider (RefreshTime). Only the seek bar's own moves seek: the player goes on from the frame, and the bar's thumb stays under the pointer while it is held, as legacy VideoSlider's did. A drag seeks at most once every 50 ms and once more on the frame it is let go on (V2-seek-bar-drag-throttle). While playing, a seek (the bar, a jump, the active Line) and the bar's release show at once, then the 100 ms pace goes on; the jumps (L, ;, Up, Down, the keyframes) go from the player's frame and play on, and next/previous frame does nothing, as in legacy. The features that read the video's frame (time from video, inserting at the video's time, the visual tools, Shift times, a tab's position) read VideoSession::reportedFrame: the shown indexed frame while paused, the player's frame while playing; a player time past the last frame's start is the last frame. The visual tools notify only when the shown frame changes their warning, and the status bar follows the texts.
- **Audio cache.** The audio box's disk cache (legacy DiskCache) is written on a writer thread of the cache's own. While a long episode's audio loaded, a slow disk had held the GUI thread in `fwrite` for 0.5 to 3 s. Frames not yet written are read from memory, and a write failure ends the open at the next block as before.

Paused frames (seek, step, edit, typing) are still prepared on the GUI thread, and indexed frames still arrive as BGRA. Qt's own video output (`VideoOutput`) is not used: the presenter composites the subtitle overlay and the visual tools over its own frame layer. Hardware-decoded frames are still read back to memory before they are kept as planes (no zero-copy texture import). Measured on Linux (2026-10-16, uncalibrated: a loaded Ryzen 5 5600, Xvfb with llvmpipe software OpenGL, Kage 07 Thai MV 1080p NV12 with its 12,296-event script, 30 s, `tmp/qt-gpu` evidence): the worker's frame conversion fell from 3.1 ms to 0.8 ms a frame, the frame upload from 8.3 MB to 3.1 MB, and the process's CPU time from 21.3 to 16.9 ms a frame; libass and overlay uploads are unchanged.

DirectShow may remain an optional Windows playback adapter and CSRI (VSFilterMod, the altqx/VSFilterMod fork's release, W2-vsfiltermod-fork; xy-VSFilter is not shipped, user 2026-10-14) an optional subtitle-renderer adapter; libass is the default renderer everywhere. They cannot dictate core types, the default cross-platform backend or a new general-purpose plugin ecosystem. Preserve a possible later macOS implementation.

## PortAudio, analysis and caches

PortAudio supplies documented **estimated** callback-buffer DAC time and stream time in a shared domain; this supports mapping ready audio to its expected audible position. It is not an observed latency, drift or acoustic-accuracy guarantee. Keep source sample frames, resampled device frames and monotonic time distinct, and report clock uncertainty. Seek, underrun, device replacement and sleep/wake invalidate stale estimates; never extrapolate stale audio indefinitely. [Audio research](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/blob/7e1aad3ba4f7ac217b9bb467a297035bc0639abd/docs/research/audio.md).

FFMS2 decode/cache workers prepare PCM and prefetch disk pages. The output callback consumes ready buffers without disk access, blocking work, allocation or QML calls. Preserve source-range audition and silence beyond its end; resampling tails, reverse/variable-speed scrubbing and optional click-suppression fades need explicit semantics and tests.

Workers produce multiresolution min/max peaks; near sample zoom can use raw samples. Render visible waveform geometry through QSG and spectrograms from cached texture tiles. Bound CPU/GPU caches; key PCM/analysis by media identity, rate/channel policy and relevant processing settings. Spectrum magnitude tiles additionally identify FFT/window/hop choices; palette changes should reuse those magnitudes. Keep cursor, selection, keyframes and karaoke handles separate so cursor motion does not recompute static analysis.

WASAPI and a Linux desktop-compatible PortAudio route are initial verification candidates, not accepted host-API guarantees. Pin the actual PortAudio revision and supported host APIs, then test Ubuntu/Fedora desktop routing; native PipeWire and ALSA/Pulse compatibility routes are different claims. Scrub gestures remain a prototype question. Device following and reopening are decided (below).

### Output device (H2)

The user's decision 60 (2026-10-16, "Follow + device picker", after the [H2 Windows session](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/blob/evidence/win-hw-20261015/evidence/win-hw-20261015/h2/results.md)) settles device policy for both audio paths, the PortAudio editor output (the audio box and its Line audition) and general playback (Qt Multimedia). The session had found that both kept the device they first opened: an audition after the Windows default changed played on the old device, neither path followed a default change during playback, a WASAPI unplug stopped the sound with "Audio player failed.", and Qt Multimedia went silent on an unplug while the video ran on (165 `AUDCLNT_E_DEVICE_INVALIDATED` lines). This is the approved departure [H2-output-device](compatibility-decisions.md).

- **Route.** `AudioOutputRouting` (application layer) resolves the route from the system's device list (`AudioDevicesPort`; in the application `QtAudioDevices` over `QMediaDevices`, whose `audioOutputsChanged` also signals a changed default) and the setting `audio.outputDevice`: the chosen device while it is connected, else the system default. It calls back only when the route changes, so a device added or removed elsewhere moves nothing. When a chosen device goes away and the default plays instead, one toast and one Log warning say so.
- **General playback.** `QtGeneralPlayer::setOutputDevice` sets the route's device on its `QAudioOutput` (`QAudioOutput::setDevice`; Qt reopens its sink and playback goes on). An id the system does not list plays through the default. The output is made once a device exists, so a host that starts without one gains sound when one arrives. On its PipeWire backend Qt does not move a stream off a removed device by itself (the Linux default on a PipeWire server; elsewhere Qt falls back to PulseAudio), so this call is what keeps the sound going there. Device ids are the sink names on both backends.
- **Editor output.** PortAudio enumerates once per `Pa_Initialize`, so `AudioBoxPlayer::switchOutput` closes the stream and rescans (`Pa_Terminate`/`Pa_Initialize`, which `PortAudioOutput::devices` does while no stream is open) before it opens again. While idle the output is closed and the next play rescans; while playing it reopens at once and goes on from the frame being heard (the clock estimate, or without one the frames the device took). A stream that ends without a stop (WASAPI's unplug, `DeviceLost`) or will not start is reopened the same way on the owner's thread; while no device opens the player waits on its frame and tries again every 250 ms and at each device change, and after 12 tries (about 3 s) it stops with legacy's "Audio player failed.". A chosen device is found by its name (the system's friendly name, which PortAudio's WASAPI and DirectSound lists share) in the host API of PortAudio's default device; while it is absent or refuses, the default plays. Linux PortAudio lists ALSA devices, not the sound server's sinks, so there the choice applies to general playback and the editor output plays through the sound server's default route, which follows the default itself.
- **Choice.** Settings > Audio > Audio output > Device lists "System default", the connected devices and a chosen device that is not connected ("Name (not connected)", so OK keeps it). OK stores the device's `QAudioDevice::id` (the endpoint id on Windows, the sink name on Linux) in `audio.outputDevice` and its name in `audio.outputDeviceName`; System default stores neither. On Windows the same group holds the editor output's API (WASAPI or DirectSound, A4-wasapi-default). The optional DirectShow adapter (W1) keeps DirectShow's own default renderer.

Evidence: `audio_devices_tests` (fake device list: default change, removal of the device in use, a chosen device missing at start and returning, the picker's entries), `audio_box_player_tests` (default change idle and playing with the samples going on from the heard frame, a lost device reopened or waited for, the chosen device by name or the default, the fill thread's recovery), `options_dialog_tests`, `general_player_tests` (with `tools/audio-removal/run.sh`, `playbackFollowsTheRouteWhenItsDeviceGoesAway` removes the playing sink of a private PipeWire or PulseAudio server in a container and listens on the remaining one; #540) and `shell_tests` `audioOutputDeviceFollowsTheSystemAndTheChoice` (the picker, persistence across runs, both paths moved by the fake list) and `audioPanelOutputDeviceMenu` (the panel menu and the notice). The user waived a new listening session after the fix (decision 60); real-device switching is observed only through the fakes above and the CI runs recorded in [coverage](coverage.md).

## Lifecycle and verification obligations

The [bounded Windows transport continuation](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/blob/4012f2ecf6cbf2ba267753c8c0f8417d2ca1f33a/HikariSub/prototypes/media-transport/README.md) records actual Qt audio/subtitle track selection, decoded frame events, FFMS2 owned frames, offscreen software presentation and 19,968 zero-filled PortAudio device frames. It supports continuing the Qt Multimedia candidate; it does not certify the pipeline. A CFR 1000-ms seek first delivered frame 24 before frame 25, and VFR 1035 ms delivered the following frame rather than its containing frame. Product acknowledgement must use delivered-frame evidence with explicitly chosen seek semantics; the probe's near-window sampling rule is not that policy.

The controlled CFR handoff rejected an intentionally delayed old-generation completion and observed software frame presentation, silent callbacks and stop/close. It did not acknowledge Qt device-buffer flushing or measure acoustic continuity. Qt and FFMS2 loaded different same-named FFmpeg DLLs in separate processes; that observation did not choose a production boundary. The later accepted [backend contract](backends.md) and [ADR 0016](adr/0016-backend-values-and-isolated-ffms2.md) select isolated FFMS2 qualification first, with Qt general playback in the application process. Transfer, recovery, loader, packaging and performance evidence remain required. Real source-PCM output, resampling, GPU/libass integration, device changes, Linux and calibrated performance also remain required. The earlier missing-prerequisite report remains historical evidence, not the current runtime state.

Serialize access to each decoder/renderer through its owner. Tag asynchronous results with source generation and relevant Document/font/configuration revisions, reject stale completions and bound queues. Define cancellation, error reporting and shutdown ordering; release graphics resources on their owning thread during window recreation/device loss. Media errors preserve subtitle editing and expose a recovery path.

Native prototypes must verify labelled controls, F6/Shift+F6 panel traversal, focus recovery, keyboard/numeric editing and screen-reader access to custom timeline state. HTML workspace observations do not establish native media accessibility.

Required evidence includes VFR/B-frame/long-GOP exact seeks; player chapters/tracks/embedded subtitles; clock handoff; loopback onset/stop tails/drift; resampling, underrun/device loss/sleep-wake; CPU/GPU color/alpha; mixed DPI and window recreation; long-track rendering/cache budgets; and clean supported-platform deployment. Audit Qt/FFMS2 FFmpeg ABI coexistence, fonts, plugins, licenses and package contents. Queue sizes, native interfaces, device policy and measured budgets must be settled by focused follow-ups under [Set measurable performance and resource budgets for the rewrite](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/issues/36) and [Choose the test strategy for the rewrite](https://github.com/altqx/hikari/issues/34). None of these gates is claimed passed here.
